## What the original question requires

The blueprint asks whether presenting the same underlying political information
in different formats or with different sourcing changes synthetic models' issue
positions, trust, certainty and factual answers. Its broader question is whether
added simulation realism provides useful predictive or inferential value. Those
are different questions from whether changing the facts changes an answer.
The owner-approved November 12 direction keeps E02 primary and the original
completion contract in force; the election's completion does not validate
synthetic responses as explanations of human voting.

## What this answers, and what remains open

We have a narrow observed wrapper null on party warmth and evidence that these
recorded model configurations can respond to directly relevant issue facts.
Available matched observations also show smaller content contrasts when an own
answer is supplied. A consistency demand, prompt compliance, repeated-answer
preference, framing or another mechanism could produce that pattern. We have
not isolated psychological anchoring or shown that the old wrapper null was
caused by prior-answer visibility. The original wrapper study and subsequent
content studies differ in outcomes, prompts, cue contents and execution dates.
They cannot be pooled into one treatment effect or used as a mediation test.

The strongest next step is therefore to cross wrapper and prior-answer
visibility while holding the actual issue text and source label constant,
using a direct issue-support measure. Party warmth remains a separately scored
secondary outcome. A text-format label test still does not establish a real
feed effect, sourcing credibility, human persuasion or realistic population
validity. Changes in synthetic outputs are not a measure of usefulness by
themselves. Added demographics, real news, assistants or swarms still need
simpler matched comparators, a defensible validation target, and the relevant
source, cost and human-comparison gates. No available result establishes that
simulation realism improves inference or election prediction.

ANES/ACS representative joint fitting, licensed real-news generalization,
trust/certainty scales, assistant mediation, swarms and a suitable matched human
experimental benchmark remain unfulfilled original requirements. Human
quantity-estimation anchoring work cited in earlier protocols is conceptual
motivation, not a human benchmark for these civic opinion prompts. This task
conducted no new literature/source acquisition or human-data analysis.
