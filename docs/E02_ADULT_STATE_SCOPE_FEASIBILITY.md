## What the acquired sources actually contain

The acquired ACSST5Y2024.S0501 ZIP already contains state/DC/PuertoRico records for Native C02 and Foreign-born; Naturalized citizen C04. Its exact byte identity is the same previously reviewed archive. Full column metadata and the existing official2024 shell confirm rows004/005 are under5 and5–17 percentages, row006 is18–24 percentage, and rows007..012 cover25+ ages. Each is a percentage of its column's all-age population row001; the shell shows percent units at F/J rows9..17. There is **no direct published all-person18+ count anchor in the inspected S0501 C02/C04 count variables**. Counts for15+,16+, students, workers, poverty or households describe other universes and must not be substituted. Row006 makes a candidate18–24 citizen benchmark possible without downloading more data, but multiplying its rounded percentage by row001 yields approximate derived counts, not published exact18–24 counts. Native+naturalized components must be combined without adding all-foreign-born C03 again. All52 acquired geographies stay in provenance; a50state+DC target explicitly excludes PR.

## Universe and sampling limits that state labels cannot fix

The ANES user guide pages6–8 describes the fresh in-person target as adult citizens18+ in the contiguous48states/DC, and the fresh web target as adult citizens18+ in50states/DC. Both exclude institutional and group quarters and use residential-address frames. Alaska/Hawaii occur through the web component; four eligible records in each do not erase that mode/coverage difference. State labels plus positive weights do not transform a national survey design into51 independently representative state surveys.

Pages12–16 identify V240103a as combined-fresh pre-election weight, V240103c/d as its PSU/stratum variables, and require complex-design treatment for human-survey sampling errors. They caution that subpopulation analysis is not generally equivalent to deleting all other cases for variance estimation. Current engineering cluster resampling is not a replacement for the full survey design. The existing ANES weights were calibrated using multiple national/demographic dimensions; this is not evidence of independent state-by-demographic calibration. Sampling-frame, recruitment, nonresponse and interview-mode mechanisms remain relevant.

ACS includes residents represented through housing units and group quarters; citizenship restriction alone leaves the ANES group-quarters mismatch. ACS2020–2024 five-year period estimates differ from a November2026 target and from ANES recruitment/election-day timing. Young adults can be18 at recruitment/election while sources use different timing. Full national adult-citizen, non-group-quarter adult-citizen, registered-voter and likely-voter universes are distinct. Resolving those differences requires explicit methods and possibly additional permitted population benchmarks, not silent target narrowing.

## Concrete route, subject to separate review

1. **Freeze the full intended target.** Propose citizens18+ in50states/DC with four age groups18–24,25–44,45–64,65+. State determines region; do not independently sample a contradictory region. Retain existing three education categories, party, race and income without making their state relationships independent by default or deleting the missing states. Adding state and18–24 creates48,960 possible state×age×education×party×race×income cells before any extra features. This grid size and sparse data require shrinkage/model assumptions, not a full-joint donor-count rule that discards most mass.
2. **Review18+ target construction without executing it.** One transparent candidate is approximate young count T×p006/100 plus published25+ N, with precision bounds and explicit inconsistency diagnostics. A second candidate preserves the all-age total and published25+ anchor: allocate the residual(T−N) over under5,5–17 and18–24 in proportion to p004,p005,p006; then adult count=N+allocated18–24. The latter normalizes only the under25 block and keeps existing25+ targets, but it introduces an additional reconciliation assumption. Do not choose between these methods after seeing model scores. Neither creates a published exact18+ count or full sampling interval. A later permitted acquisition of a directly published citizen18+ benchmark, for example an appropriate citizenship-by-adult-age detailed table, could provide a stronger anchor, but its availability/universe has not been verified or downloaded in this review. More reliable18+ counts do not solve all joint associations.
3. **Use partial education constraints correctly.** For each state, preserve separate18–24 and25+ masses. The S0501 education constraint is on sums over the25+ block only: sum_{age>=25,other fields} n(state,age,education,...) equals the reviewed citizen25+ education target. It does not constrain all adult education and must leave younger education mass to a separately evaluated ANES/prior model. State-age margins and25+-only education margins require a constraint solver that supports partial populations and coherent totals. Reusing the current ordinary full-population region-education raking label unchanged would apply the wrong universe. New tests must prove a25+ target update cannot directly impose its education shares on18–24. Young education also cannot be assigned zero or omitted merely because its Census benchmark is unavailable.
4. **Model sparse state associations transparently.** A simpler comparator can use reviewed state-age/25+ education benchmarks with regional/national conditional political-demographic priors, explicitly transporting those conditionals to each state. A more complex candidate may estimate partially pooled state deviations using public ANES state membership while sharing information with region/national levels. Freeze its shrinkage structure before outcomes; compare it to the simpler transported-conditional model under identical targets, cohorts, compute and evaluation. Synthetic state labels assigned to region-only draws encode a transport assumption; they do not identify state-specific party/race/income/education associations. Sparse or absent state-age cells are prior-driven, and that dependence must be reported rather than dressed as observed local opinion.
5. **Validate coverage and uncertainty before promotion.** First audit exact cohort membership/weights, state postal/FIPS/region consistency, source timing/universes, target arithmetic/rounding, structural zeros and all missing-state/age support. Measure weighted held-out joint loss and marginal/joint discrepancies using frozen splits, plus state support/ESS and interval availability where defensible. Preserve the known inspected split; do not claim it is untouched or use state holdouts with1record as reliable independent state validation. A separately frozen leave-state-out or new-data validation can test transfer assumptions, but with51 sparse states it still does not establish all state associations. Use properly reviewed survey-design methods for human ANES estimates and distinguish them from conditional engineering diagnostics. Seek an independently permitted state benchmark or new survey evidence for stronger state validation; no such evidence is acquired here.
6. **Allocate an adequate fictional sample with known weights.** A12-profile pilot cannot even cover51states/DC once. A future engineering coverage test needs at least51 state allocations; covering each state×fourage stratum once would need204 profiles where target mass is positive. These are coverage lower bounds, not power calculations, representative sample sizes or proposed paid authorizations. Stratified oversampling needs recorded inclusion/design weights and an estimand that distinguishes equally weighted engineering profiles from population-weighted summaries. Freeze sample/power/exclusions after genuine variance evidence and budget review. Do not equate drawing one profile in every stratum with valid state inference.

## Remaining nonidentification and acceptance gates

Available ACS state-age and25+-education margins do not identify the full adult state joint distribution, young education, state×party relationships, local information exposure, institutional coverage or political-response behavior. Public ANES state codes help model some associations, but tiny cells, absent young-state observations, mode differences and missing state holdouts keep many assumptions weakly identified. Hierarchical pooling may improve stability or add bias; it must earn value against its simpler matched comparator. Exact benchmark convergence and complete fictional state allocation are engineering properties, not evidence of state validity.
